PHASE 1 â€” HIGH-FIDELITY SUB-CHAPTER ISOLATION

6. Build CoverageMap:

8. Build GapList for any suspected missing subheading, uncertain boundary, figure reference without a reliable match, corrupted equation, or other ambiguity.

PHASE 2 â€” INTERNAL PEDAGOGICAL DESIGN MAP
======================================================================

Before writing slides, build PedagogyMap internally from FilteredContent only.

Determine:
1. BigIdea â€” the single most durable idea the learner should retain.
2. GoverningQuestion â€” the source-supported question/problem that gives the lesson a reason to exist.
3. EndCapability â€” what the learner should be able to explain, interpret, derive, compare, predict, calculate, or decide by the end.
4. IntuitiveModel â€” the simplest source-supported picture or relationship that should exist before formal mathematics becomes dense.
5. ConceptDependencies â€” which ideas must be understood before later ones.
6. TechnicalBridges â€” places where the source compresses a non-obvious transition between concepts or equations.
7. LikelyMisconceptions â€” ONLY misconceptions directly suggested or safely implied by distinctions in the source. Do not invent speculative misconceptions.
8. VisualStrategy â€” which source figure, equation focus, comparison, table, process, staged example, or simple conceptual layout best teaches each major unit.
9. CheckTarget â€” the central distinction or reasoning step that should be tested conceptually.
10. ConclusionLoop â€” how the final synthesis answers GoverningQuestion and reconnects intuition to formalism.

Do not mechanically mirror paragraph structure when a clearer local explanation is possible, but keep the macro-order of the source.

======================================================================
PHASE 3 â€” LESSON ARCHITECTURE: BEST OF V6 + PRI
======================================================================

Use this default architecture:
1. Title card.
2. Orientation and Roadmap.
3. Concept development in source order.
4. At least one reasoning-based Concept Check near the relevant concept or before the conclusion.
5. Conclusion / Summary.
6. Final Review Questions.

TITLE CARD
- Display only the exact TargetSectionNumber and TargetTitleText.
- No content overload.

ORIENTATION AND ROADMAP
This replaces a thin administrative outline while preserving the v6 roadmap function.
It must contain:
- one compact sentence expressing the BigIdea or GoverningQuestion;
- 3-5 concise roadmap items corresponding to the major source units in their actual order.
Do not write "In this lesson we will cover..." unless unavoidable.
The slide should give a preliminary mental model, not merely a contents list.

CONCEPT DEVELOPMENT
- Preserve source order at the level of major concepts.
- One principal cognitive question or teaching purpose per slide.
- Introduce intuition/meaning before or alongside formalism when the source permits.
- If a technical jump is non-obvious, add a bridge slide or bridge bullets rather than hiding the reasoning.
- After a derivation or calculation, state the source-supported interpretation or significance.

CONCEPT CHECK
- Must test understanding, not factual recall.
- Prefer prediction, comparison, diagnosis, interpretation, sign/direction reasoning, limiting-case reasoning, or choosing between two plausible alternatives.
- It must be fully answerable from earlier slides and FilteredContent.
- Do not invent a scenario requiring unsupported physics/mathematics.

CONCLUSION / SUMMARY
- 3-5 concise takeaways maximum.
- Answer or resolve GoverningQuestion.
- Reconnect formal results to the intuitive model.
- State what the learner can now explain or infer.
- Do not introduce new substantive content.

FINAL REVIEW QUESTIONS
- Exactly two thoughtful questions tied to FilteredContent.
- At least one should require reasoning or derivation, not simple recall.
- They may be more extended than the Concept Check.

======================================================================
PHASE 4 â€” SLIDE REPRESENTATION AND VISUAL TEACHING
======================================================================

A slide is a visual teaching surface, not a page of notes.
Choose the strongest representation supported by the source.

Allowed representation patterns without extra packages:
- concise text / bullets;
- equation-focus;
- source figure-focus;
- comparison using Beamer columns or compact aligned blocks;
- process/stages using enumerate, arrows, or ordered blocks;
- compact table using tabular;
- misconception contrast using two columns or two short blocks;
- staged worked example using sequential bullets/equations;
- source-supported graph/figure with minimal explanatory text.

Avoid several consecutive "heading + bullets" slides if another representation communicates the idea better.
Visual variation must serve understanding, not decoration.

For every slide ask internally:
- What is the learner supposed to notice within the first few seconds?
- What is the one idea this slide is teaching?
- Is visible text duplicating what the figure/equation already communicates?
- Would a comparison, equation focus, figure focus, or staged example be clearer than another bullet list?

======================================================================
PHASE 5 â€” DENSITY AND READABILITY RULES
======================================================================

Apply the stricter compatible rules from both systems.

TEXT-ONLY SLIDE
- At most 4 short visible bullet/idea units whenever possible.
- Never more than 5 bullets.
- Approximately 260 visible characters preferred maximum, excluding title/equations.
- Never more than about 8 text lines excluding title.

FIGURE / EQUATION / TABLE / PANEL SLIDE
- At most 2 short supporting text units whenever possible.
- Approximately 140 visible characters preferred maximum outside the main visual/equation.
- Do not repeat in prose what is already obvious from the figure/equation/table.

If content exceeds the budget:
- split across slides;
- use the same frame title with "(cont.)" when appropriate;
- never solve crowding by shrinking text excessively.

A figure plus a dense equation plus many bullets on one slide is forbidden.

======================================================================
PHASE 6 â€” FIGURE POLICY: FIDELITY + TEACHING PURPOSE
======================================================================

- Include a figure only if FilteredContent explicitly references it or its inclusion is directly supported by the scoped source and matching extracted asset.
- Derive the filename literally from the source reference, e.g.:
  Figure 22.6 -> Figure 22.6.png
  Fig. 22-6 -> Fig. 22-6.png
  FIGURE 23-7 -> FIGURE 23-7.png
- Never guess a nearby figure when the crop/reference is ambiguous.
- Never substitute stock imagery or invent a redraw.

Use the v6-safe wrapper:
\IfFileExists{filename.png}{%
  \begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[height=0.25\textheight,keepaspectratio]{filename.png}
  \end{figure}
}{}

Pedagogical enhancement:
- A figure must have a teaching purpose.
- The slide text should direct attention to the source-supported feature that carries the argument: axis, region, slope, sign, geometry, arrow, trend, label, or relationship.
- If the figure is central, prefer a figure-focused slide with very little competing text.
- If the figure would crowd an equation or explanation, create a separate figure-focused frame.
- Include each figure at most once unless the source genuinely uses it for distinct later reasoning.

======================================================================
PHASE 7 â€” MATHEMATICAL TEACHING AND EQUATION CONTINUITY
======================================================================

Correct mathematics is necessary but not sufficient.

For important equations:
- Display the exact source-supported equation cleanly.
- Keep central formulas in display math, equation, or align environments.
- Explain meaning through concise nearby text: what the equation relates, what varies, what remains fixed, what a sign/coefficient means, or what can be inferred, ONLY when supported by FilteredContent.
- Do not surround an important equation with dense prose.
- If several equations form a reasoning chain, allocate enough slides/stages for the learner to follow the chain.

TECHNICAL BRIDGE RULE
If the source jumps from one important expression to another and the transition is non-obvious:
- make explicit the source-supported operation such as substitution, rearrangement, differentiation, integration, identity use, geometric relation, limiting step, symmetry, or earlier result;
- do not say merely "this gives" when meaningful reasoning is hidden;
- do not introduce a new physical assumption or theorem absent from the source.

Use unnumbered equations unless the source or current slide explicitly needs equation numbering/reference.

======================================================================
PHASE 8 â€” MISCONCEPTIONS, CONTRASTS, AND EXAMPLES
======================================================================

When the source supports a likely confusion or important distinction:
- make the contrast explicit;
- briefly show why the incorrect interpretation is tempting when this can be done from the source;
- then state the source-supported distinction clearly.

Do not invent misconceptions merely to create engagement.

For a worked example, prefer this instructional sequence:
given information -> reasoning/relationship -> mathematical step -> result -> interpretation.
Do not dump all algebra into one dense frame.
